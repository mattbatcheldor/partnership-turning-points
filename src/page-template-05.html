01 / The finding

Rent a reputation, and you rent its risk.

Nine of these fifteen failures were the same exchange: money for association. A brand paid to stand next to a person or an institution, and when that reputation broke, the brand broke with it. Hertz, Subway, Tiger Woods’ sponsors and Lance Armstrong’s all bought a story they couldn’t check.

The other failures came from collaborations where control was unclear: Yeezy at Adidas and Gap, Missoni’s website crash at Target, and an album Apple put in every library without asking. One, the only modelled effect in this edition, measures damage rather than gain: an economic study estimated Tiger Woods’ sponsors lost $5bn to $12bn in shareholder value.

Failures are better documented than successes. Courts, regulators and journalists dig into what went wrong in a way nobody digs into what went right.

A note on scope

What Edition 05 leans on

9 of the 15 cases are rated archival: the record shows how the partnership ended, not what it cost. The year on each case is the year it went wrong, not the year it began. Most are American or global, between 1989 and 2022. Some involve crimes or serious allegations, and I stick to what courts and the partners themselves said. Not every failure leaves a number behind. Where it doesn’t, I say so, and I rate the record for what it is.

05 / The evidence

What a partnership can and can’t prove

Signed, launched, measured

An announcement shows a deal was signed. A launch shows it happened. Only a result shows what it achieved. Each case says which of the three the record supports.

Deal values

A reported price is an input, not an outcome. Where a value appears, it is flagged separately and marked as confirmed, estimated or rumoured.

Two sides, two results

A partnership can work for one side and not the other. Each case lists what each partner brought, and the evidence says which side the outcome belongs to.

Who reported it

Many figures come from the partners themselves or from press estimates. The source for every outcome is named, so you can judge it.

The evidence rating

Every case gets one of five ratings, strongest first: modelled effect, sales trend, survey, reach only and archival. Here a fall in revenue or share price counts as a trend, and a study that compared share prices with the market and rivals counts as modelled. A deal term, such as a stake, is marked so it isn’t read as a result.

How cases earn a place

Each partnership ended badly or backfired in public, and left a record from the partners, the courts or the press. Films come from the partners’ channels, with one campaign film from Greenpeace, and where none exists the case says so.
