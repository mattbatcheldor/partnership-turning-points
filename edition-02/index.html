01 / The finding

Proof comes from partnerships with a job.

Eight of these fifteen partnerships publish a modelled effect, against none of the famous ones in Edition 01. In every one, a partner gave the partnership a job it could measure: card spending for Mastercard, party food sales for Iceland, watching with friends for Heineken, loyal customers for O2.

The proof is almost always one-sided. The papers measure what the sponsor gained, and say little about AEG, Channel 4 or the Rugby World Cup. Pampers and UNICEF is the exception the other way round: the outcome for the charity is well documented, and the return for the brand stays private.

Effectiveness papers are written to prove that something worked, so this edition shows what partnerships can achieve, not what is typical.

01 2007Sponsorship and placement / O2’s name and customers, AEG’s arena O2 × AEG / 2007 / Money for associationThe O2A phone network turned a national joke into the world’s most popular music venue.Sponsorship and placement · Signed, launched and measured 14.5:1Return on marketing investmentModelled effect

London / Mobile networks and live entertainment

IPA 2010 · Gold · VCCP for O2

The situation

The Millennium Dome had become a national embarrassment. AEG was reopening it as an entertainment venue and needed a partner, and O2 wanted something that would keep its customers loyal.

The exchange

O2 put its name on the venue and built its customer offer around it. Customers got Priority Tickets before anyone else, and the venue got a famous name from day one.

  • O2 Its name, millions of customers and the money to market the venue.
  • AEG A reopened arena and a programme of the biggest acts in music.

The turning point

The O2 opened in 2007. In its first seven months it drew 3.4 million visitors, generated £61m worth of PR, and a million O2 customers signed up for Priority Tickets.

My take

The benchmark every sponsorship pitch I have seen gets measured against. It worked because the customer got something real, not just a logo on a roof.

What the evidence shows

Two IPA papers track the sponsorship. The first, in 2008, reported a 26:1 return a year after launch. The second, a Gold in 2010, projected a £639m contribution to profit from new customers over the communications’ lifetime. Both are O2’s own models, written up for an effectiveness award.

What to take into a deal

Don’t badge a venue, build your customer offer into it. The 2010 paper calls this breathing rather than badging.

Global / Airlines

Y&R London for Star Alliance

The situation

Frequent business travellers wanted to fly anywhere on one ticket and one loyalty scheme. No single airline could cover the world.

The exchange

Each airline brought its routes, lounges and loyalty members. Together they offered a network none could offer alone.

  • The member airlines Routes, lounges, loyalty schemes and planes.
  • The alliance One brand and one promise to travellers across all of them.

The turning point

Star Alliance launched in 1997. Advertising ran in airports and business titles, and some planes were repainted to match.

My take

The most unusual partnership in this edition: rivals agreeing to share a brand. It only works because travellers cared more about the network than the logo.

United Kingdom, then many markets / Nappies and child health

Pampers and UNICEF

The situation

Maternal and newborn tetanus was killing babies in dozens of countries. Pampers wanted parents to see it as more than a nappy.

The exchange

For each specially marked pack sold, Pampers funded a tetanus vaccine through UNICEF. In Ireland, for example, that meant €0.078 a pack.

  • Pampers Packs in millions of homes, and money per pack.
  • UNICEF Vaccination programmes, and trust with parents.

The turning point

The campaign began in 2006 and ran for years across many markets. By 2014 it had helped provide 300 million doses of tetanus vaccine.

My take

The clearest proof in the edition is for the charity, not the brand. That’s the right way round, but it means Pampers’ own return stays private.

Northern Ireland / Tourism and television

IPA 2024 · Publicis London and OMD UK for Tourism Ireland

The situation

Game of Thrones and Derry Girls were filmed in Northern Ireland and had made people want to visit. The tourism agencies didn’t have full rights to use the shows’ footage.

The exchange

The agencies worked with the programme makers to create their own experiences, such as a Game of Thrones tapestry and TV sponsorship in the style of Derry Girls characters.

  • Tourism Ireland Marketing budgets and the places themselves.
  • The programme makers Stories, characters and fans around the world.

The turning point

The work ran as visitors returned after the pandemic, and aimed to turn viewers into visitors.

My take

A partnership built around a limitation. The tapestry exists because the rights didn’t, and it became a tourist attraction itself.

05 / The evidence

What a partnership can and can’t prove

Signed, launched, measured

An announcement shows a deal was signed. A launch shows it happened. Only a result shows what it achieved. Each case says which of the three the record supports.

Deal values

A reported price is an input, not an outcome. Where a value appears, it is flagged separately and marked as confirmed, estimated or rumoured.

Two sides, two results

A partnership can work for one side and not the other. Each case lists what each partner brought, and the evidence says which side the outcome belongs to.

Who reported it

Many figures come from the partners themselves or from press estimates. The source for every outcome is named, so you can judge it.

The evidence rating

Every case gets one of five ratings, strongest first: modelled effect, sales trend, survey, reach only and archival. Here a rise in sales or company value counts as a trend. A deal term, such as a stake, is marked so it isn’t read as a result.

How cases earn a place

Each partnership has a published effectiveness paper or a documented outcome. Fourteen come from the IPA Effectiveness Awards. Films come from brand, agency or tourism channels, and where none exists the case says so.
