01 / The finding

Rent a reputation, and you rent its risk.

Nine of these fifteen failures were the same exchange: money for association. A brand paid to stand next to a person or an institution, and when that reputation broke, the brand broke with it. Hertz, Subway, Tiger Woods’ sponsors and Lance Armstrong’s all bought a story they couldn’t check.

The other failures came from collaborations where control was unclear: Yeezy at Adidas and Gap, Missoni’s website crash at Target, and an album Apple put in every library without asking. One, the only modelled effect in this edition, measures damage rather than gain: an economic study estimated Tiger Woods’ sponsors lost $5bn to $12bn in shareholder value.

Failures are better documented than successes. Courts, regulators and journalists dig into what went wrong in a way nobody digs into what went right.

A note on scope

What Edition 05 leans on

9 of the 15 cases are rated archival: the record shows how the partnership ended, not what it cost. The year on each case is the year it went wrong, not the year it began. Most are American or global, between 1989 and 2022. Some involve crimes or serious allegations, and I stick to what courts and the partners themselves said. Not every failure leaves a number behind. Where it doesn’t, I say so, and I rate the record for what it is.

Global / Sportswear and music

Adidas and Ye, 2015 to 2022

The situation

Adidas and Kanye West, now known as Ye, launched Yeezy in 2015. By 2022 it was one of the most valuable collaborations in sportswear, and the shoes carried his name and design.

The exchange

Ye designed the shoes and lent his name. Adidas made, marketed and sold them around the world.

  • Adidas Design teams, factories and global retail.
  • Kanye West The designs, the name and a huge following.

The turning point

In October 2022, after a series of antisemitic statements by Ye, Adidas ended the partnership and stopped production. It was left holding a warehouse of shoes with his name on them.

My take

Adidas did the right thing, and it still cost a fortune. The lesson isn’t to avoid big partners. It’s to know what you own when it ends.

United States / Soft drinks and pop music

Pepsi and Madonna, 1989

The situation

Pepsi had followed Michael Jackson with Madonna. Her new single, ‘Like a Prayer’, would premiere in a Pepsi advert before anyone heard it on the radio.

The exchange

Pepsi paid Madonna a reported $5m. Madonna gave Pepsi the first play of her new single, in a gentle advert about childhood memories.

  • Pepsi A reported $5m and a global advertising slot.
  • Madonna A new single and the attention of the world.

The turning point

The advert aired in early 1989. The next day Madonna released her own video for the song, with burning crosses and religious imagery. Religious groups threatened a boycott, and Pepsi pulled the advert.

My take

Madonna kept the money and the controversy. Pepsi kept neither, until it decided, decades later, that the story was worth telling.

United States / Car rental and sport

Hertz and O.J. Simpson, 1975 to 1994

The situation

Hertz wanted business travellers to see renting a car as fast and easy. O.J. Simpson was one of the most popular athletes in America.

The exchange

Hertz paid Simpson to star in its adverts, sprinting through airport terminals to the Hertz counter. Simpson gained fame far beyond football.

  • Hertz Money and national television campaigns.
  • O.J. Simpson Speed, charm and celebrity.

The turning point

In June 1994, Simpson was arrested for the murders of Nicole Brown Simpson and Ron Goldman. Hertz cut its ties with him. He was acquitted in 1995 and later found liable in a civil case.

My take

The most successful endorsement of its era became the hardest to watch. Nothing about the adverts changed, only what people knew.

New York and Zurich / Payments and football

Mastercard and FIFA, 1990 to 2006

The situation

Mastercard had sponsored the World Cup for sixteen years and believed it had the first right to renew. FIFA was negotiating with Visa at the same time.

The exchange

Mastercard paid for its place as the World Cup’s card. FIFA gave it the association with the world’s most watched tournament.

  • Mastercard Sponsorship money and sixteen years of loyalty.
  • FIFA The World Cup.

The turning point

In 2006 FIFA signed Visa. Mastercard sued in New York, and a judge ruled that FIFA had broken its agreement and misled both companies. In 2007 FIFA settled, Visa kept the rights and Mastercard walked away with a payment.

My take

Mastercard won in court and lost the World Cup. Sometimes the best outcome of a broken partnership is a cheque and a clean exit.

United States / Breakfast cereal and swimming

Kellogg’s and Michael Phelps, 2008 to 2009

The situation

After winning eight gold medals at the 2008 Beijing Olympics, Michael Phelps was on cereal boxes across America.

The exchange

Kellogg’s paid Phelps to appear on its packs and in its adverts. Phelps gave Kellogg’s the most celebrated athlete of the year.

  • Kellogg’s Money and a place on the breakfast table.
  • Michael Phelps Eight gold medals and a clean, wholesome image.

The turning point

In February 2009 a British newspaper published a photo of Phelps with a bong. Kellogg’s said his behaviour was not consistent with its image and let his contract lapse. The next day Subway said it would keep him.

My take

Kellogg’s acted on its image, Subway on its customers. History has been kinder to Subway’s call.

United States / Golf and consumer brands

Victor Stango and Christopher Knittel, UC Davis, 2009

The situation

Tiger Woods was the richest athlete in the world, sponsored by Nike, Gatorade, Accenture, AT&T, Gillette and EA Sports, among others.

The exchange

The sponsors paid Woods for his image of discipline and success. Woods gave them the most dominant athlete in sport.

  • The sponsors Hundreds of millions in endorsement fees.
  • Tiger Woods Winning, and an image of control.

The turning point

After a car crash outside his home in November 2009, reports of infidelity followed. Accenture, AT&T and Gatorade ended their deals. Nike stayed.

My take

The only modelled effect in this edition measures damage, not gain. That tells you something about what gets studied.

United States / Retail and fashion

Target and Missoni, 2011

The situation

Target had made cheap designer collaborations its signature. Missoni, the Italian fashion house, was known for colourful zigzag knitwear.

The exchange

Missoni designed some 400 products, from clothes to bikes. Target made them affordable and sold them in its stores and online.

  • Target Stores across America and low prices.
  • Missoni A famous pattern and Italian fashion credibility.

The turning point

On launch day in September 2011, Target’s website crashed and stayed down for most of the day. Shelves emptied within minutes, and the products appeared on eBay at several times the price.

My take

The only failure here caused by too much love. Customers weren’t angry at Missoni. They were angry they couldn’t get it.

Nike, Trek, Anheuser-Busch, Oakley and Lance Armstrong

The situation

Lance Armstrong had survived cancer and won seven Tours de France. Nike, Trek, Anheuser-Busch and Oakley built campaigns on his story, and Livestrong sold millions of yellow wristbands.

The exchange

Sponsors paid Armstrong for his image of courage. Armstrong gave them the most inspiring story in sport.

  • The sponsors Money, products and campaigns.

The turning point

In October 2012 the US Anti-Doping Agency published its evidence of doping. On 17 October Nike ended its contract, saying it had been misled for more than a decade. Trek and Anheuser-Busch followed the same day, and Oakley days later.

My take

Nike said it had been misled. The harder question for every sponsor is how much they wanted to believe.

Global / Toys and oil

LEGO and Shell, 1966 to 2014

The situation

LEGO had made Shell-branded sets since 1966, and in the 2010s they were sold at Shell petrol stations in 26 countries. Shell was planning to drill for oil in the Arctic.

The exchange

LEGO made Shell-branded toys. Shell sold them at its stations and put LEGO in front of millions of families.

  • LEGO Its bricks and the trust of parents.
  • Shell Petrol stations and a promotion budget.

The turning point

In July 2014 Greenpeace released a film showing a LEGO Arctic drowning in oil. In October, LEGO said it would not renew the deal, while saying the issue was between Greenpeace and Shell.

My take

LEGO insisted the campaign wasn’t about it. It ended the deal anyway, because a brand built on trust can’t afford the argument.

Gap and Ye, 2020 to 2022

The situation

Gap was searching for relevance. In 2020 it signed a ten-year deal with Kanye West, now known as Ye, who had worked at a Gap store as a teenager.

The exchange

Ye designed collections for Gap. Gap manufactured and sold them, and expected the line to earn $1bn within five years.

  • Gap Stores, manufacturing and a mass audience.
  • Kanye West Designs and cultural pull.

The turning point

In September 2022 Ye’s lawyer told Gap he was ending the deal, saying Gap had broken its promises on stores. Gap said the partners were not aligned. Weeks later Gap pulled the remaining products from stores after Ye’s antisemitic statements.

My take

Gap wanted culture, Ye wanted control. Neither got what they signed for.

05 / The evidence

What a partnership can and can’t prove

Signed, launched, measured

An announcement shows a deal was signed. A launch shows it happened. Only a result shows what it achieved. Each case says which of the three the record supports.

Deal values

A reported price is an input, not an outcome. Where a value appears, it is flagged separately and marked as confirmed, estimated or rumoured.

Two sides, two results

A partnership can work for one side and not the other. Each case lists what each partner brought, and the evidence says which side the outcome belongs to.

Who reported it

Many figures come from the partners themselves or from press estimates. The source for every outcome is named, so you can judge it.

The evidence rating

Every case gets one of five ratings, strongest first: modelled effect, sales trend, survey, reach only and archival. Here a fall in revenue or share price counts as a trend, and a study that compared share prices with the market and rivals counts as modelled. A deal term, such as a stake, is marked so it isn’t read as a result.

How cases earn a place

Each partnership ended badly or backfired in public, and left a record from the partners, the courts or the press. Films come from the partners’ channels, with one campaign film from Greenpeace, and where none exists the case says so.
